1. Getting Started in the Course

Description

An introduction to the essential information students need before beginning the course, including how the module components fit together. It sets expectations for navigating weekly work and knowing where to find key details.

Subtopics

  • What You Need to Know to Get Started — This module orients you to the structure of the course before you begin your first week of work. Knowing how the pieces fit together helps you plan your time and avoid surprises.
  • Weekly Assignments (60% of Your Grade) — Weekly assignments make up the largest portion of your grade at 60%. They are the primary way you demonstrate your learning each week.
  • Weekly Graded Discussions (20% of Your Grade) — Each week includes a graded discussion worth a combined 20% of your grade. Discussions have stricter timing than assignments because they serve a second purpose.
  • Quizzes (20% of Your Grade) — There are 12 quizzes across the course, together worth 20% of your grade. They are designed to reinforce the material you work with in your assignments.
  • Navigating Your Weekly Work — Each week follows a predictable rhythm so you always know what to do next. Building a routine around the weekly cycle keeps you on track.
  • Getting Help and Contacting Your Instructor — Support is available whenever you encounter problems or have questions about the course. Communicating early is the best way to stay on track.

2. Weekly Assignments (60% of Grade)

Description

Weekly assignments make up the largest portion of the final grade at 60%. Students should attempt each assignment by its due date, though the instructor is flexible and understanding when difficulties arise.

Subtopics

  • Weight of Weekly Assignments in the Final Grade — Weekly assignments are the single largest graded component of this course, accounting for 60% of your final grade.
  • Weekly Cadence and Due Dates — Assignments are released and due on a weekly basis, giving the course a predictable rhythm.
  • Attempt Every Assignment — The expectation is that you make a genuine attempt at every weekly assignment, even if you are unsure of the answers.
  • Flexibility When Difficulties Arise — The instructor is flexible and understanding if circumstances prevent you from completing an assignment by the due date.
  • Getting Help with Assignments — If you run into problems or have questions about an assignment, reach out to the instructor directly by email.
  • How Quizzes Reinforce Your Assignments — The 12 course quizzes are designed to support the material covered in the weekly assignments.

3. Weekly Graded Discussions (20% of Grade)

Description

A weekly graded discussion accounts for 20% of the final grade and must be submitted before the posted due date. These discussions also serve as the official record of attendance, so late posts are not accepted.

Subtopics

  • Weight in the Final Grade — Weekly graded discussions make up 20% of your overall course grade.
  • Strict Due Dates — Unlike weekly assignments, discussions must be completed prior to the posted due date.
  • Discussions Serve as Attendance — Your discussion post is the official record that you attended the course that week.
  • How Discussions Differ from Assignments — The flexibility offered for weekly assignments does not extend to graded discussions.
  • Getting Help with Discussions — If you run into problems or have questions about a discussion, contact the instructor by email.

4. Quizzes (20% of Grade)

Description

Twelve quizzes together account for 20% of the final grade and have no fixed due dates. They are designed to reinforce and support the material covered in the weekly assignments.

Subtopics

  • Quiz Weight in the Final Grade — Quizzes make up 20% of your overall course grade. They are one of three graded components alongside weekly assignments and graded discussions.
  • Twelve Quizzes Across the Course — The course includes a total of twelve quizzes spread across the term. Each one targets a specific portion of the course material.
  • No Fixed Due Dates — Unlike discussions, quizzes do not have set due dates. This gives you flexibility in deciding when to complete them.
  • Quizzes Support the Assignments — Quizzes are designed to reinforce the material covered in the weekly assignments. They serve a learning function, not just an assessment function.
  • Recommended Approach to Pacing — Because quizzes are self-paced, a deliberate strategy helps you stay on track. Aligning quizzes with the weekly assignment cycle is the most effective approach.
  • Getting Help with Quizzes — If you encounter problems or have questions about a quiz, reach out to the instructor. Email is the preferred channel for support.

5. Grading Breakdown Summary

Description

A consolidated look at how the three graded components—assignments, discussions, and quizzes—combine to form 100% of the course grade. This topic helps students prioritize their effort across deadline-driven and self-paced work.

Subtopics

  • The Three Graded Components — Your final course grade is built from three categories that together total 100%. Knowing the weight of each helps you decide where to invest your time.
  • Assignments: The Largest Share at 60% — Weekly assignments represent the majority of your grade, making them the highest priority each week. Consistent completion here has the greatest impact on your final score.
  • Discussions: 20% and Deadline-Driven — Weekly graded discussions are worth 20% of your grade and are the only component with firm deadlines. They also serve a second purpose beyond points.
  • Quizzes: 20% and Self-Paced — Twelve quizzes make up the final 20% of your grade and have no due dates. They are designed to reinforce the material covered in your assignments.
  • Prioritizing Deadline-Driven vs. Self-Paced Work — Because the three components have different deadline rules, a simple weekly strategy helps you stay on track. Handle fixed deadlines first, then flexible work, then self-paced work.

6. Getting Help and Communicating with the Instructor

Description

Guidance on reaching out when problems or questions arise during the course. Email is the designated channel for contacting the instructor for support.

Subtopics

  • Email Is the Primary Contact Channel — When problems or questions come up during the course, email is the designated way to reach the instructor.
  • When to Reach Out — Contact the instructor as soon as an issue affects your ability to participate or complete work.
  • Communicating About Late or Missed Work — The instructor is flexible with weekly assignments, but communication is what makes that flexibility possible.
  • Writing an Effective Message — A clear, specific email helps the instructor respond quickly and accurately.
  • Asking Questions Early — Raising questions early prevents small problems from affecting your grade later in the term.
